# Typed directive supersession (T1050)

The sender, not the daemon's prose classifier, supplies an operation `directive_family` and `directive_kind`. A family is a caller-chosen, exact-match scope such as `report/T1050`; kinds are `authorization` and `hold`. This contract is opt-in: old untyped messages cannot safely be treated as permissions merely because their text mentions permission. To protect a workflow, its authorizations **must** use typed `jevons_event_push` and its holds may use typed `jevons_event_push` or `jevons_agent_send`. `jevons_agent_send` rejects typed authorization because its busy fallback cannot yet preserve metadata. No implicit matching of the event name, actor or prose occurs.

A later hold cancels **pending** authorizations for the same recipient and family, preserving unrelated FIFO traffic and authorizations for other families. A hold before a subsequent authorization permits that new authorization. A busy event hold enters the queue in the same transaction that removes older permissions; a direct hold removes them before invoking the provider. The removed entries, including their original text and ids, are archived under `sendq/supersessions/<hold-id>.json`, readable with `Store.ReadSupersession`. The hold is not accepted if a matching authorization is Attempting or Uncertain: its delivery may already have begun, so a hold cannot honestly claim to supersede it. Reconcile that entry before retrying the hold. A queue claim and a hold serialize under one store lock; no stale Pending entry can be claimed after an accepted hold. Typed entries do not enter queue digests, which would otherwise destroy their identity.

**Boundary:** this cannot revoke an authorization already in a provider's transcript or queue. The sending workflow must wait for an unresolved attempt to be reconciled and must not interpret an errored hold as delivered. Direct delivery may itself be unconfirmed after the pending authorization has been removed; that is fail-closed for permission, but the sender must establish the hold reached the recipient before claiming the recipient was instructed. The queue's per-agent lock protects concurrent calls in one daemon process; this does not provide distributed multi-daemon locking. Do not apply these fields to a live owner report as an experiment.

## Open owner policy gate (independent audit)

The observed 05:11:33 busy `jevons_event_push` owner authorization and 05:12:06 direct plain-text `jevons_agent_send` hold were **both untyped**. The old permission remains pending and can drain after the hold under this implementation. `TestT1050UntypedIncidentStillUnsafe` exercises both actual MCP entry points in that ordering without sending a real report; `TestT1050TypedBusyEventAndDirectAgentHold` shows the same paths protected only after both producers supply the structured contract. The API cannot distinguish these two instructions from unrelated messages without guessing from prose. Closure requires an owner decision specifying the authoritative producer(s) and a mandatory structured operation/family contract for **both** sides, followed by producer migration and an end-to-end test of those actual entry points. Until then the opt-in implementation is a capability, not protection for the historical workflow. Do not claim T1050 achieved from the typed tests alone.

A prepared supersession archive records an interrupted transaction and `ReadSupersession` refuses to call it committed; only a successful queue save and audit finalization records committed status. Two independently opened `Store` instances are not serialized by the current process-local mutex. Running two daemons against one sendq directory is outside the current locking contract: the operator must ensure exclusive daemon ownership before relying on this feature. An interprocess lock (applied consistently to append, claim, resolve and directive operations) would be needed to support concurrent writers, not merely a lock in `ApplyDirective`.
